# LinkedIn Post — Style Guide

The voice we want: **a senior engineer talking to other engineers about something they actually did.** Curious, specific, honest. Never marketing.

## Structure (most posts)

```
HOOK (1–2 lines)
  Drop the reader into a concrete moment, decision, or surprising number.
  No "I want to share...", no "Today I learned...".

CONTEXT (3–5 lines)
  What is the actual problem? Why does it matter? Numbers help here.

TURN (2–4 lines)
  The non-obvious thing. The bit a casual reader wouldn't have guessed.
  This is where the reader thinks "huh, I'll remember that."

LANDING (2–3 lines)
  One sentence the reader can apply to their own work.
  Don't pitch the app. The post itself does the marketing.

(Optional) ASIDE (1 line)
  A self-aware line about what you almost did wrong, or what you'd do differently.
```

Total length: 8–15 lines on LinkedIn (which displays ~3 lines before "see more"). The first two lines are everything — they decide whether someone expands.

## Voice rules

- **First person, singular.** "I", not "we" — solo founder, own it.
- **Past tense for decisions.** "I built", "I removed". Reads as a story, not a sales sheet.
- **One idea per post.** Pick the sharpest cut and trust it.
- **Specific over general.** "₹0.40/month per user" beats "very cheap to run". "GPT-4o-mini" beats "AI model".
- **Show the tradeoff.** Real engineering decisions involve giving something up. Name it.

## What to never write

- "🚀"
- "Excited to share..."
- "Building in public..."
- "Hot take:"
- "Unpopular opinion:"
- "Most people don't realize..."
- "Game-changer"
- "Lessons learned" (as the post title — show them, don't label them)
- Any em-dash chain longer than two
- Rhetorical questions in bold ("Want to know why?")

## What to lean on

- A specific bug + the moment you understood it
- A number you were surprised by
- A choice between two options where you picked the boring one
- Code that's good *because* it's small
- A user behavior that taught you something about the product
- "I almost did X. I'm glad I didn't, because Y."

## Formatting

- No emojis at the start of a line.
- No "🧵" or "thread" — LinkedIn isn't Twitter.
- Use line breaks generously. Each paragraph is 1–3 lines max.
- If you use a code snippet, keep it under 6 lines. Otherwise, link to the repo.
- Don't use bullet lists for prose. Save bullets for genuine enumeration.

## Hook patterns that work

- **The contrarian fact.** *"My Indian nutrition app costs me ₹0.40/user/month to run."*
- **The decision moment.** *"I deleted 467 lines of code last week. The app got better."*
- **The number nobody expected.** *"GPT-4o-mini handles 200 nutrition lookups for less than a cup of chai."*
- **The bug that taught you something.** *"My drawer wasn't opening on Android. The drawer was opening on Android. I had to be sure of both before I saw the answer."*
- **The opposite of what you'd expect.** *"I built a credit system, shipped it, then ripped it out. Here's why."*

## CTA — usually none

The best posts don't have a CTA. If the post is good, the comments come.

If you must include one, it goes in the last line and is *not* a sales pitch. Examples that are OK:
- *"What would you have done instead?"*
- *"If you've shipped a similar fix, I'd love to read it."*

Examples that are not OK:
- *"Try Nourish today!"*
- *"DM me to learn more"*
- *"Link in bio"*

## Length guide

- **Standard post:** 800–1500 characters.
- **"Big idea" post:** up to 3000 characters, but the first 200 still have to earn the click.
- **Image post:** caption is short, 300–600 chars, image carries the explanation.

## Sign-off

No sign-off. The end is the end.
